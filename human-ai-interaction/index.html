Human-AI Interaction

Problem Methods Findings Recommendations Impact Presentation

Problem

Live captioning is very cognitively demanding task, such that self-reported measures found captioning to be more stressful than surgery.

To mitigate these demands and improve not just working conditions of live captioners in broadcast environments but also viewing outcomes of Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing audiences, a paradigm shift was proposed.

Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) has already replaced captioners in online meetings and post-production events. As such, it follows that AI/ASR based captioning using modern LLMs can improve live captioning when performed with a human captioner.

Methods

Modern LLMs are trained on massive datasets and act as probablistic engines-- they are biased towards the majority and choose the most statistically likely token to output. Marginalized populations, such as Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing audiences, make up a much smaller share of the data and their needs and voices are diminished by this probablistic reasoning so a Human-in-the-Loop is required-- not just for evaluation and checking, but as experts of their craft who use AI as a tool. As a reminder, LLMs rely on text, sound and images-- they cannot replicate human learning as a multi--sensory experience (I have written about this elsewhere).

Executed a study drawing from psychology, cognitive science, computer science and industrial engineering to conduct a study where captioners supervised AI to write captions rather than working alone

Mixed-methods approach with 21 captioners using NASA-TLX, System Usability Scale, Nielsen-Norman Satisfaction Scale, Trust in Automation Scale and qualitative interviews

Time-series-- 3 sessions spaced 24 hours apart to allow for learning

Controlled environment and pre-screened for familiarity

Two independent groups: Novices and Experts-- controlled for skill level

Findings

Mitigating fear is about expectation management

Captioners desire more control over AI

AI reduced physical and mental workload over just 3 sessions spaced 24 hours apart

Recommendations

Human benefit from control over AI

Even if AI can automate the job, manual control should be relegated

Scheduling is one recommended solution

Another is interface based (see Design Mock-up from the side menu)

Why involve a human captioner at all? Research shows that human+AI working conditions produce the best results, and captioning is more than just transcription.

Captioning is the art of understanding what information needs to be conveyed to the audience and requires cultural understanding, human judgement and first-hand experience serving Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing audiences.

Impact

Led the only study on captioners supervising AI in the world

Broadcasting agencies and governments are not prepared for this inevitable shift so I took matters into my own hands. I established Captioners as Supervisors of AI, promoting them from a role of operator to a role of delegator and judge. By combining human and AI talent, we get the best of both worlds: the tireless efficiency of AI and the priceless judgement and experience of humans.

Discovered new relationships between Human-AI constructs and made novel contributions

Developed a reproducible and transferrable model for all AI assisted workflows to guide superior Human-AI Interfaces and maximize the potential of Human-AI collaboration. The future is Human-AI and I can help you implement evidence-based solutions.

Presentation

The culmination of my work was presented to key stakeholders, including the CEOs of 3PlayMedia and ScribeWire, representatives from broadcasters and providers (i.e Rogers) and advocacy groups

Successfully defended at Toronto Metropolitan University and presented to C-level executives, technical and non-technical audiences.
